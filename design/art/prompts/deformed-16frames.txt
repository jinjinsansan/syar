WHAT A HORSE GALLOP IS (this matters — a previous attempt looked like a cheetah)
- The gallop is a FOUR-BEAT gait. The four legs land one at a time, never as pairs.
- There is exactly ONE airborne moment per stride, and during it the legs are
  FOLDED UNDER THE BODY, not stretched fore and aft.
- The classic "flying gallop" pose (all four legs straight, front reaching forward
  and hind reaching back, in mid-air) does NOT occur in a real horse. Muybridge's
  1878 photographs disproved it. Do not draw it.
- Each leg straightens twice per stride: reaching forward just before it lands, and
  pushing back just before it leaves the ground. In between it is folded.
